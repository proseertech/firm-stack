# Initial-Return Checklist (Year 1 and New Clients)

Run when the Initial Return box is checked, this is the corporation's first S year (including a C-to-S conversion), or this is a new client whose prior returns the firm did not prepare. Many first-year positions are made by *how the first return is filed* rather than by a separate statement — and several are irrevocable. Confirm each decision was deliberate, that the return reflects it, and that a statement is present **only where one is required**.

Terminology and box-code conventions follow the Terminology section of `SKILL.md`.

## The S election itself

- **Form 2553 and the acceptance letter** — Sight both. Confirm the effective date covers this tax year and that all required shareholder consents were signed by the persons who were shareholders on the election date.
- **Late election** — If filed late, confirm the Rev. Proc. 2013-30 relief language is on the Form 2553 and that the reasonable-cause statement is in the file. Do not assume relief was granted; the acceptance letter is the evidence.
- **Effective date vs. return period** — A mid-year election means a short S year and a short C year, with two returns. Confirm both exist and that the income split follows the election date.
- **Shareholder eligibility on day one** — Individuals who are US citizens or residents, estates, qualifying trusts (grantor, QSST, ESBT, voting, or testamentary within its two-year window), and qualifying exempt organizations. For a trust shareholder, the QSST or ESBT election is the trust's obligation and its own filing — confirm evidence is in the file rather than assuming it.
- **One class of stock from inception** — Read the charter, articles, bylaws, and any shareholder or buy-sell agreement for provisions conferring different rights to distribution or liquidation proceeds. Check shareholder loans against the §1361(c)(5) straight-debt safe harbor.
- **State S election** — Several states require a separate election or recognize the federal election only on notice. Route to Preparer Questions per the federal-only scope constraint.

## C-to-S conversion carryovers

Run this section whenever the corporation existed as a C corporation before the election, or acquired assets with a carryover basis from a C corporation.

- **NUBIG established and documented** — The net unrealized built-in gain at the conversion date drives the §1374 exposure for five years. Year 1 is when the appraisal or valuation support is obtainable; reconstructing it in year four is expensive and weak. Confirm an asset-by-asset conversion-date schedule exists. See `former-c-corp-regimes.md`.
- **AE&P carried forward** — Accumulated earnings and profits from the C years survive the election and drive distribution ordering and the §1375 passive income tax. Confirm the opening AE&P figure ties to the final C-corporation return and is disclosed on Schedule B.
- **LIFO recapture (§1363(d))** — A C corporation using LIFO must recapture the LIFO reserve on its **final C return**, with the tax payable in four annual installments. Confirm the recapture was reported and the installment schedule is being followed.
- **Carryforwards that do not survive** — C-corporation NOLs, capital loss carryovers, and general business credit carryforwards generally cannot offset S-corporation pass-through income (though a C NOL can offset the §1374 BIG tax base). Confirm they were not swept into the S return.
- **Opening AAA is zero** — AAA starts at zero on conversion regardless of the retained-earnings balance on the books. A nonzero opening AAA on a converted entity means the balance sheet was imported as the tax account.

## Tax year and accounting method

- **Tax year** — A calendar year, a permitted year, or a §444 election. If §444: Form 8716 filed, and the annual Form 8752 required-payment obligation noted for future years. A business-purpose fiscal year requires a Form 1128 ruling — confirm which basis applies.
- **Accounting method** — The method used on this return *is* the election. Confirm cash vs. accrual is permissible under §448 (aggregate gross receipts at or below the §448(c) threshold on an aggregated basis, and not a tax shelter) and that it matches how the books are kept or the M-1 explains the difference.
- **Tax-shelter check, run once and applied twice** — A corporation allocating more than 35% of losses to shareholders who are limited entrepreneurs is a syndicate, therefore a tax shelter. That single conclusion both forces accrual accounting and disqualifies the corporation from the §163(j) small-business exception. A year-1 return on the cash method that also skips Form 8990 is internally inconsistent; resolve which conclusion is right before sign-off.
- **Inventory method** — If inventories exist: method adopted, including the §471(c) small-taxpayer book-conformity method. LIFO requires Form 970 filed with this return. Confirm Form 1125-A is attached.

## Start-up, organizational, and stock-issuance costs

Do **not** look for a §195 or §248 election statement — the regulations deem the election made by return treatment. Test classification, mechanics, and deliberateness instead.

- **Three-way classification** — Every pre-operating and formation cost lands in exactly one bucket. A single "start-up and organizational costs" line with no split is a finding.
  - **§195 start-up** — Investigatory and pre-opening operating costs of the trade or business.
  - **§248 organizational** — Costs incident to the creation of the corporation, chargeable to capital account, and of a character that would be amortized over a limited life: incorporation fees, organizational meeting costs, temporary-director expenses, and the legal and accounting fees of organizing.
  - **Stock-issuance / capital-raising costs** — Costs of issuing or selling stock, printing certificates, underwriting and placement fees, and the legal and accounting fees attributable to the offering. These are **capital costs that reduce paid-in capital**: not deductible, not amortizable, no 180-month schedule. Amortized stock-issuance costs are a HIGH finding, and they are routinely swept into the §248 bucket.
- **§195 mechanics** — Up to $5,000 deducted, reduced dollar-for-dollar to the extent start-up expenditures exceed $50,000; remainder amortized over 180 months beginning with the month the **active trade or business begins**. Deemed elected under Reg. §1.195-1(b).
- **§248 mechanics** — Up to $5,000 deducted, same $50,000 phase-down; remainder amortized over 180 months beginning with the month the **corporation begins business**. Deemed elected under Reg. §1.248-1(c).
- **Confirm on the return** — The $5,000 (as limited) and the 180-month amortization actually appear, and each schedule starts in its own correct month. Incorporation date, business-start date, and the date the active trade or business begins can all differ; a schedule keyed to the incorporation date or defaulted to January is a finding.
- **Capitalization is a decision** — Forgoing the deemed election is **irrevocable** and applies to **all** costs in the category. If the return capitalizes, confirm it was deliberate and documented; if it looks like a data-entry default, flag it while the return is still amendable in the year of election.

## Election statements — confirm attached where required

- **De minimis safe harbor** (Reg. §1.263(a)-1(f)) — annual election statement if the fixed-asset policy relies on it.
- **Bonus depreciation elect-out** (§168(k)(7)) — statement by class; silence means bonus applies.
- **§179** — Form 4562, Part I; check the limit, phase-out, and the entity-level taxable-income limitation, and note each shareholder faces a separate limit.
- **§163(j)(7)(B) electing real property trade or business** — common in year 1 for real estate S corporations and **irrevocable**. It requires ADS for nonresidential real property, residential rental property, and QIP; confirm the first-year fixed-asset register was actually set up on ADS, because a later correction is a method change.
- **§174A** — if domestic R&E is being capitalized and amortized rather than expensed, document the election in year 1.
- **Reg. §1.1367-1(g)** — the basis-ordering election (losses before nondeductible non-capital expenses) binds later years; if it is being made, the statement belongs with this return.
- **§444** — Form 8716, if a fiscal year was elected.

## Basis and equity accounts

- **Initial stock basis** — Built from actual contributions: cash plus the adjusted basis of contributed property, with §351 and §357 consequences confirmed if property came in subject to debt. Basis that starts wrong stays wrong every year after, and no later return will surface it.
- **Debt basis** — Only bona fide indebtedness running directly from the shareholder to the corporation. Confirm the note, terms, and payment history exist from the start; a guarantee of third-party debt creates nothing until the shareholder pays.
- **Entity-level liabilities are not basis** — Unlike a partnership, corporate debt does not enter any shareholder's basis. If the opening basis workpaper includes a share of corporate liabilities, it was built on the wrong model.
- **Form 7203 inputs** — Establish the schedule the shareholders will need from year one; it is their form but the corporation's data.
- **Opening AAA, OAA, and retained earnings** — AAA and OAA start at zero. Beginning retained earnings on Schedule L should reflect only a predecessor's carryover where one exists; otherwise a nonzero opening balance means the books imported another entity's history or the Initial Return box is wrong.
- **Stock ledger** — Confirm one exists and agrees with the K-1 percentages. Per-share-per-day allocation is unverifiable without it, and year 1 is when it is easiest to establish.

## First-year consistency checks

- Depreciation conventions (half-year vs. mid-quarter — a year-end asset spree triggers mid-quarter) and short-year depreciation if the first year is short.
- Bonus depreciation rate matched to each asset's **acquisition** date for 2025 additions (40% before January 20, 2025; 100% on or after), including assets acquired under a pre-January 20 written binding contract.
- Officer compensation exists and is on payroll from the point services began. A first-year return with material net income and no wages sets a pattern that compounds; flag the exposure now.
- >2% shareholder health premiums and other fringes set up correctly in payroll (W-2 Box 1) from the start (§1372).
- First-year §199A setup: how many separate trades or businesses, the SSTB determination and its reasoning (noting that **engineering and architecture are excluded** from SSTB), and whether rental income treated as QBI meets the §162 or Rev. Proc. 2019-38 requirements from the outset.
- Filing mechanics for a first return: due date, Form 7004 if extended, e-file eligibility (a newly issued EIN is a common e-file rejection), Form 1125-A if there is inventory, Form 1125-E at total receipts of $500,000 or more, and the Schedule B total-receipts/total-assets test for Schedules L and M-1 — with **Schedule M-2 required regardless**.
- State registrations and first-year state filings match the operating footprint — route to Preparer Questions per the federal-only scope constraint.
